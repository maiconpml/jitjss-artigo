\begin{abstract}
The Just-In-Time Job Shop Scheduling (JITJSS) problem is a challenging combinatorial optimization problem where operation completions outside specific due dates incur earliness or tardiness penalties.
The objective is to minimize the total weighted sum of these penalties across all operations.
Because tardiness penalties dominate total schedule costs, we propose a novel domain-specific neighborhood operator, \texttt{swap\_crit}, which traces individual backward critical paths of tardy operations to swap bottleneck operations at critical block boundaries.
To validate the effectiveness of the proposed operator without designer bias, we embed \texttt{swap\_crit} within an algorithmic design space parameterized via a context-free grammar in Backus-Naur Form, coupling it with alternative literature heuristics, metaheuristic frameworks, and exact sequence timing sub-solvers.
Using the \texttt{irace} configurator as an objective validation testbed on a dedicated training set, the automated design process converged to an Iterated Tabu Search architecture that decisively selected \texttt{swap\_crit} in 80\% of the elite configurations.
In computational experiments on the standard 72 literature benchmark instances, the resulting algorithm matched or improved upon state-of-the-art results across 72.2\% of the scenarios.
Pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistically significant improvements over established baselines ($p < 0.05$), with our collective search establishing 25 new best-known solutions.
These results demonstrate that the proposed critical-path operator addresses key structural bottlenecks in JITJSS and establish Automatic Algorithm Design as a powerful methodology for validating novel algorithmic components.
\end{abstract}
